How to calculate your GPA

Every course carries a number of credit hours, and every grade maps to grade points on the 4.0 scale. A course with more credit hours pulls your GPA harder, which is why a plain average of your grades is not the same as your GPA. The calculator above does the arithmetic for you; this is what happens behind the button.

SGPA: one semester

SGPA (Semester Grade Point Average) is the credit-weighted average of the grades in a single semester.

SGPA = Σ(credit hours × grade points) ÷ Σ(credit hours)

  1. Multiply each course's credit hours by its grade points. The result is the quality points for that course.
  2. Add up the quality points of all courses.
  3. Add up the credit hours of all courses.
  4. Divide total quality points by total credit hours.

CGPA: all semesters

CGPA (Cumulative Grade Point Average) combines every completed semester. Each SGPA is weighted by the credit hours of its semester.

CGPA = Σ(SGPA × semester credits) ÷ Σ(semester credits)

  1. Multiply each semester's SGPA by its total credit hours.
  2. Add up these results for all semesters.
  3. Add up the credit hours of all semesters.
  4. Divide the first total by the second.

Worked example: SGPA

Four courses in one semester
Course Credits Grade Grade points Quality points
Calculus I 3 A 4.00 12.00
Physics 4 B+ 3.33 13.32
Programming 3 A- 3.67 11.01
English 2 B 3.00 6.00
Total 12 42.33

SGPA = 42.33 ÷ 12 = 3.5275, which rounds to 3.53.

Worked example: CGPA

Two completed semesters
Semester SGPA Credits SGPA × credits
Semester 1 3.40 18 61.20
Semester 2 3.70 15 55.50
Total 33 116.70

CGPA = 116.70 ÷ 33 = 3.5364, which rounds to 3.54. Notice that this is not the plain average of 3.40 and 3.70 (3.55), because Semester 1 had more credit hours.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between SGPA and CGPA?

SGPA covers one semester. CGPA covers every semester you have completed, with each semester weighted by its credit hours. Use the SGPA tab for a single semester's courses and the CGPA tab to combine semester results.

What is considered a good GPA?

It depends on your program and goals. On a 4.0 scale, 3.0 is generally a solid pass level, 3.5 and above is strong, and scholarships or graduate programs often set their own minimum. Check the requirement of the specific program you are aiming for.

Will this match my university's official GPA?

It will if your university uses the same 4.0 scale shown above. Some universities use slightly different grade values or round differently, so treat the result as an accurate estimate and confirm it against your transcript.

Why do credit hours matter?

A 4-credit course counts more than a 2-credit course. Weighting by credit hours makes sure your GPA reflects how much work each course represents.

How do I handle a repeated course?

Policies differ: some universities replace the old grade with the new one, others average both. Follow your university's rule, or enter the SGPA and credit values exactly as they appear on your transcript in the CGPA tab.

Are my grades saved or uploaded anywhere?

No. The calculation runs in your browser, and the courses and grades you type are not sent to a server.
